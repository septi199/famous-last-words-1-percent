# Notes — why this line

Entry: "One percent. I have survived a thousand drops, a toilet, and your pocket, and this is how I go — dimming, like a celebrity leaving a party early."

Why this one, against the judge's criteria in order:

1. **Last words a phone says at 1 percent.** The speaker is the phone,
   the moment is the battery hitting 1 percent, stated in the first
   two words.
2. **A real twist.** The phone is not sad, it is offended: it survived
   drops, a toilet, a pocket — and this undramatic ending is beneath
   it. The celebrity-exit image is the punch, landing on the last word.
3. **The phone's own voice.** It talks about its own body (drops,
   pocket, dimming screen) and its own history, not a person's
   feelings about losing a phone.
4. **Tight.** One line, every clause earning its place: the list of
   survivals sets up the indignity, the final image pays it off.
5. **Fresh.** The brief warns against the stock moves — the will
   reading, the goodbye to the charger, the pun on 1 percent. This
   line does none of them: no will, no charger mentioned, and "one
   percent" is a fact, not a pun.

Deliberately avoided: "tell my charger I loved it", any will-style
bequest ("my apps go to..."), and "1% battery, 99% problems" wordplay.
